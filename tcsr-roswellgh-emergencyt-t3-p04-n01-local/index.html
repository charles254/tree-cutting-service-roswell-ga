Common Fungal Pathogens: Phytophthora, Hypoxylon & Leaf Scorch

Tree decline frequently stems from subsurface fungal pathogens attacking the root system or vascular cambium. Hypoxylon canker attacks drought-stressed oaks, while Phytophthora root rot proliferates in waterlogged clay soils, suffocating feeder roots.

  • Phytophthora root rot causing sudden canopy chlorosis and basal trunk weeping

Deep Root Bio-Stimulation & Georgia Clay Aeration Protocols

Heavy clay soils easily become compacted, suffocating tree root respiration. Arborists use pneumatic air spades to aerate compacted root plates without damaging fine feeder roots, injecting mycorrhizal fungi and liquid bio-stimulants deep into the root zone.

  • Pneumatic air-spade soil decompression relieving root collar compaction
  • Deep root liquid injections of slow-release nitrogen, potassium, and micronutrients
  • Inoculating soil with beneficial mycorrhizal fungi to enhance water uptake
  • Remediating root flares buried under damaging residential mulch volcanoes
